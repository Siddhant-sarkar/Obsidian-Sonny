# Verb conjugation

Verbs are conjugated in German. This means they change forms according to the number of people they refer to. Unlike English, where you only have to add an _-s_ to verbs in the third person singular _(he learns)_, there are more changes in German. The verb’s basic form (to learn) is called the infinitive.

**Infinitiv**: _Lernen, machen, sprechen, kennenlernen._  
Depending on the rest of the sentence, you sometimes need to add **zu**:  
**zu** lernen, **zu** machen, **zu** sprechen, kennen**zu**lernen.  
[For compound verbs like _kennenlernen, zu_ is placed in the middle of the verb. You’ll learn about compound verbs in a later lesson.]

To form the **first person singular** (_I learn_), you need to remove the  _-n_  from the infinitive:  
_ich lerne, ich mache, ich spreche._

To form the **second person singular** (you learn), you need to remove _-en_ from the infinitive and add  _-st_:  
_du lernst, du machst, du sprichst._

In addition, you need to change a vowel for certain verbs:  
_du ~~sprechst~~ sprichst._

The forms of _sein_ (_to be_) are irregular:  
_ich bin, du bist._

# Third Person Conjugation

To form the **third person singular** of verbs _(he/she/it learns_), you need to remove  _-en_  from the infinitive and add  _-t_:  
_er/sie/es lernt, er/sie/es macht, er/sie/es spricht_

If verbs undergo a vowel change, it’s the same for the second and third person singular:  
_du sprichst, er/sie/es spricht_  
_du gefällst, er/sie/es gefällt_

# Der, Die, Das
 
 Depending of the gender of the noun, use _der/die/das_ for _the_ (the definite article) and _einer/eine/eines_ for _a_ (the indefinite article) Don’t let yourself get intimidated by the German articles. You will get the hang of them. For now, just focus on these lessons and studying with Anki.

# Perfekt

The standard way of expressing the past in German is using the “**Perfekt**”. Just like the English present perfect, it consists of two parts: the present verb form of _haben_ according to the person, and the past participle **(Partizip Perfekt)**. The past participle is formed by adding _ge-_ to the beginning of the infinitive and  _-t_  or  _-en_  to the end. In some cases, certain letters change (_denken -> gedacht)._

_Ich habe gesagt_

_Du hast gemacht_

_Er/Sie/Es hat gehabt_

_Wir haben gesprochen_

_Ihr habt gedacht_

_Sie haben getrunken_

Keep in mind that, if there is an object, it is placed between the form of “haben” and the past participle.

_Ich habe Englisch geübt._

_Wir haben Spaß gehabt._

Other parts of the sentence can also be placed in between. But don’t worry too much about German word order for now. It will come naturally to you in due time.

# Perfekt Mit sein
_For certain verbs, sein is used as the auxiliary verb instead of haben to build the perfect form. These verbs usually refer to movement.

Ich bin gefahren
Du bist gegangen
Er/Sie/Es ist gewesen
Wir sind gelaufen
Ihr seid angekommen
Sie sind aufgestanden

# Prateritum
In addition to the “Perfekt”, Germans also use another past tense construction: the “**Präteritum**” which is similar to the English simple past. The “**Perfekt**” stresses the **result** of past actions in the present, while the “**Präteritum**” is used for recounting past actions **without any connection to the present**. However, the “Präteritum” is used a lot less than the English simple past, and **in spoken language, its use is limited to a few common verbs**. For these, you should always use the “Präteritum” but for all others, use the “Perfekt”. These verbs are mainly modal verbs but there are also a few others:

_Haben – ich hatte_
_Sein – ich war_
_Müssen – ich musste_
_Können – ich konnte_
_Wollen – ich wollte_

_Dürfen_ (be allowed to) – _ich durfte_
_Sollen_ _– ich sollte_
_Werden (become) – ich wurde_
_Denken – ich dachte_
_Gehen – es ging_ (only for the neuter, in all other cases, the perfect forms are used)

_Wissen – ich wusste_
_Finden – ich fand_

As you can see, _-te_  is added to the infinitive and in most cases vowel changes take place. Don’t worry about the rules of how to build these verb forms. Just keep studying with Anki and you’ll be familiar with them soon enough.

The verb conjugation in the “Präteritum” is very similar to the present:

_Ich musste_
_Du musstest_
_Er/Sie/Es musste_
_Wir mussten_
_Ihr musstet_
_Sie mussten_

# Akkusativ
We use the accusative case to indicate a phrase’s direct object (the aim of the verb). This is the person or thing affected by the action of the verb. For example:

_Max trinkt **ein Bier**._ (Max drinks a beer.)  
**_Ein Bier_** is an accusative form. The beer is affected by the action of drinking.  
_Sie mag_ **_ihn_**. (She likes him.)  
_**Ihn**_ is an accusative form. He is a person affected by the action of liking.

If you are whether you need an accusative form, you can ask yourself questions in the following manner:

_**Was** trinkt_ _Max?_ **Ein Bier**. (**What** does Max drink? **A beer.**)  
**_Wen_** _kennt sie?_ **_Ihn._ (Whom** does she know? **Him**.)

Most accusative forms are the same as the standard nominative forms (the forms you’ve encountered so far). Only some of the singular masculine forms differ. In the case of masculine nouns, the definite article changes from _der_ to _den_ and the indefinite article changes from _ein_ to _einen._ And in certain cases, you also add _-n or -en (der Junge -> den Jungen, ein Student -> einen Studenten_).

| Maskulinum | Femininum                | Neutrum                 |                    |
| ---------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------ |
|            |                          |                         |                    |
| Nominativ  | der Junge, ein Junge     | die Schule, eine Schule | das Bier, ein Bier |
| Akkusativ  | den Jungen, einen Jungen | die Schule, eine Schule | das Bier, ein Bier |
In plural, all accusative forms are the same as nominative forms.

|               |                          |
| ------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Nominativ** | die sympathischen Männer |
| **Akkusativ** | die sympathischen Männer |
|               |                          |
If there’s a masculine form of an adjective, you always add _-en_.

|               |                            |
| ------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Nominativ** | der sympathische Mann      |
| **Akkusativ** | den sympathische**n** Mann |

Some prepositions require the use of the accusative, e.g. _ohne_ or _in_ as a direction.

_Ohne **den Menschen** hat die Erde keine Probleme.  
__Der Junge geht in_ **_die Schule_**.

The accusative is also used in expressions like _jeden Tag, den ganzen Tag, Guten Morgen!_

# Dativ
In German, there’s another important case beside the accusative, the **dative**. It is used to indicated the **indirect object** of a phrase. This means it indicates to or for whom an action is taken. To express this in English, you usually need a preposition:

_Give it **to me**!_ vs. Gib es **mir**!

Certain verbs like _helfen (to help)_ always require an **indirect** object, but a lot of verbs combine an **indirect** with a direct object:

_Er hilft **ihr**.  
__Vielleicht schreibe ich **dir** einen Brief.  
Hast du **mir** etwas mitgebracht?_

The personal pronouns have the following forms:

| **Nominativ** | **Dativ** | **Akkusativ** |
| ------------- | --------- | ------------- |
| ich           | mir       | mich          |
| du            | dir       | dich          |
| er            | ihm       | ihn           |
| sie           | ihr       | sie           |
| es            | ihm       | es            |
| wir           | uns       | uns           |
| ihr           | euch      | euch          |
| sie           | ihnen     | sie           |

Nouns have the following dative forms:
## Singular

|           | Definite article | Indefinite article |
| --------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Masculine | dem Film         | einem Studenten    |
| Feminine  | der Frau         | einer Uni          |
| Neuter    | dem Haus         | einem Buch         |

## Plural

|                           | Definite article | Indefinite article |
| ------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Masculine/feminine/neuter | den Leuten       | Leuten             |

That’s a lot of forms, but don’t get overwhelmed. Start off by just trying to recognize the dative forms. You’ll soon get a sense of how they should be used.

Certain prepositions require the dative case. The most common ones are _aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von_ and _zu_ i.e.

_mit der Post, nach dir (after you), bei mir, von der Uni_.

_In_ and _an_ can be followed by both the accusative or the dative, depending on whether you are talking about a location (dative) or a direction (accusative).

_Ich bin in **der Schule**._ vs. _Ich gehe in die Schule._

_An dem_ and _in dem_ usually contract to form _am_ and _im._

_in dem Garten_ –> _im Garten_

# Konjunktiv II (subjunctiv II)
In German, the **Konjuktiv II** is used either as a **polite way of asking for something**:

_Ich hätte gerne eine Schnitzel mit Pommes._ (I would like a schnitzel with fries.)

For **suggestions**:

_Ich würde Ihnen den Lachs empfehlen._ (I would recommend the salmon to you.)

Or to express **hypothetical statements**:

_Ach, das wäre nicht so schlimm. Wir hätten bestimmt auch etwas Anderes gefunden.  
_(Oh, that wouldn’t be too bad. We would have found something else for sure.)

Its usage is therefore very similar to the English conditional (would + verb).

There are present and past forms of the Konjunktiv II, just like the English conditional (I would do vs. I would have done). E. g.

## Present form:

_Ich würde das Auto kaufen._ (I would buy the car.) (And I could still buy it.)

## Past form:

_Ich hätte das Auto gekauft._ (I would have bought the car.) (But someone else bought it already.)

In the case of most verbs, the **present forms of the Konjuktiv II** are formed by _würde_ + infinitive. _I would have_ is Ich _hätte_ instead of _ich_ _würde haben_ and _I would be_ becomes _ich wäre._ Note that most of the verbs which use the Präteritum as the regular past tense (see lesson 11) don’t use the _würde_ form:

_Haben – ich hätte  
__Sein – ich wäre  
__Müssen – ich müsste  
__Können – ich könnte  
__Dürfen_ (be allowed to) – _ich dürfte  
__Sollen_ _– ich sollte  
__Werden (become) – ich würde  
__Gehen – es ginge_ (only for the neuter, in all other cases, the _würde_ forms are used)  
_Wissen – ich wüsste  
__Finden – ich fände_

The **past forms of the Konjunktiv II** are formed by _wäre/hätte +_ Partizip Perfekt.

_Ich hätte gemacht  
__Ich hätte gewusst  
__Ich wäre gegangen  
__Ich wäre gewesen_

# Steigerung von Adjektiven
The comparative _(younger)_ and superlative forms _(youngest)_ in German are very similar to those in English:

_(young, younger, youngest -> jung, jünger, am jüngsten)._

Simply add _-er_ for the comparative and _am …-sten_ for the superlative.

Some adjectives undergo vowel changes for comparison _(alt -> älter)_ and a handful of them have irregular forms _(gern, lieber, am liebsten)._

If the superlative is an adverb, _am_ has to be used in conjunction with it:

_Sein Bruder schläft am meisten._

In a phrase with _sein_ you can use _am_ or an article

_Ich bin der jüngste._ vs. _Ich bin am jüngsten._

These comparison forms still act as adjectives and will take on the respective ending, depending on the word they are modifying:

# Reflexive Verben
A verb is reflexive, if someone does something to or for himself.  You need to use reflexive pronouns (_myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves)_ together with the verb. German has quite a few reflexive verbs which aren’t reflexive in English. For example:

_sich anziehen (to get dressed): Sie zieht sich an._ (She gets dressed.)  
_sich beeilen (to hurry): Beeil dich!_ (Hurry up!)  
_sich leisten (to afford): Kann ich mir das leisten?_ (Can I afford that?)

The reflexive pronouns in German are as follows:
| Person    | Reflexivpronomen Dativ | Reflexivpronomen Akkusativ |
| --------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------- |
| ich       | mir                    | mich                       |
| du        | dir                    | dich                       |
| er/sie/es | **sich**               | **sich**                   |
| wir       | uns                    | uns                        |
| ihr       | euch                   | euch                       |
| sie       | **sich**               | **sich**                   |

Note that the reflexive pronoun is always _**sich**_ for the third person, no matter whether it’s singular or plural, dative or accusative. This is also the only form where the reflexive pronouns differ from the personal pronouns. Here’s a quick review of the personal pronouns (lesson 14)::

| Nominativ | Personalpronomen Dativ | Personalpronomen Akkusativ |
| --------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------- |
| ich       | mir                    | mich                       |
| du        | dir                    | dich                       |
| er        | ihm                    | ihn                        |
| sie       | ihr                    | sie                        |
| es        | ihm                    | es                         |
| wir       | uns                    | uns                        |
| ihr       | euch                   | euch                       |
| sie       | ihnen                  | sie                        |

You will soon develop a sense for which verbs require the dative for their reflexive pronoun and which ones require the accusative. Since a lot of the forms are the same, it doesn’t matter in many cases. If you want to know anyway, these are the most common ones which require an **accusative**:

_sich erinnern – du erinnerst dich  
__sich fühlen (feel) – du fühlst dich  
__sich setzen – du setzt dich  
__sich aufregen – du regst dich auf  
__sich anziehen – du ziehst dich an  
__sich entschuldigen – du entschuldigst dich  
__sich freuen – du freust dich  
__sich beeilen – du beeilst dich  
__sich entspannen – du entspannst dich  
__sich melden – du meldest dich  
__sich streiten – du streitest dich_

And these ones require a **dative**:  
_sich ansehen – du siehst dir an  
__sich leisten – du leistest dir  
__sich wünschen – du wünscht dir  
__sich Sorgen machen – du machst dir Sorgen  
__sich vorstellen (imagine) – du stellst dir vor  
__sich Mühe geben (put effort into sth.) – du gibst dir Mühe_

The reflexive pronouns are also used in cases where you’d say _each other_ in English:

_Sie kennen sich. (They know each other.)_

# Komposita
You can build **compound verbs** by adding prepositions (_zu, an, auf, aus,…)_ or other prefixes _(zurück, heim, ein,…)_ in front of verbs:

_machen: zumachen, aufmachen, anmachen, ausmachen  
__gehen: hingehen (go there), zurückgehen, heimgehen  
__schlafen: einschlafen  
__sehen: ansehen (look at), aussehen (look like)  
__stehen (stand): aufstehen  
__hören: zuhören (listen)  
__suchen: aussuchen (pick)  
__kommen: ankommen  
__rufen (call): anrufen (call by phone)_

However, in order to build sentences with these compound verbs, you sometimes have to separate them – hence the name **separable verbs (trennbare Verben)**:

_Er hört zu.  
__Sie geht heim._

The **object** of the sentence is always placed **in between**:

_Sie macht das Licht aus.  
__Sie macht die Tür zu._

In the _**Perfekt**_, these verbs are **not separated**:

_Sie ist angekommen.  
__Sie ist eingeschlafen._

If there’s an infinitive with _zu, zu_ is placed between the verb and its prefix:

_Sie hasst es, früh aufzustehen.  
__Ich habe mich entschieden, dich anzurufen._

# Futur
The present tense is usually used to talk about the future but there is also a future tense. It is built with **_werden_ plus infinitive**:

_Sie werden heiraten_ (they will marry)  
_Es wird regnen_ (it will rain)

You can use it to stress the future aspect of a phrase. It is also used in certain fixed expressions e.g.

_Wir werden sehen._ (We’ll see.)  
or  
_Das wirst du noch bereuen._ (You will regret this.)

For assumptions, _**schon**_ or _**wohl**_ is often added.  
_Das wird **schon** gehen. Es wird **schon** nichts passieren._ (That’ll be fine. Nothing will happen.)  
_Wenn du das sagst, wird das **wohl** stimmen._ (If you say that, it’ll be true.)

# Genitiv
The genitive case is used to indicate belongings or relations. It’s not as common in spoken language where you often use _von_ + dative instead:

Standard: _Der Bruder des Mannes ist arm._ (The man’s brother is poor.)  
Colloquial: _Der Bruder von dem Mann ist arm_. (The man’s brother is poor.)

For **singular masculine** or **neuter noun**s _–**s**_ or _–**es**_ is added in most cases. You should stick with, _-s_ which is always right; ­_-es_ is not possible in some cases. Singular feminine nouns don’t change at all. The genitive forms are the following:

## Singular

|            | Bestimmter Artikel      | Unbestimmter Artikel      |
| ---------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Maskulinum | des großen Mannes/Manns | eines großen Mannes/Manns |
| Femininum  | der großen Frau         | einer großen Frau         |
| Neutrum    | des großen Hauses       | eines großen Hauses       |

## Plural

_Er hört zu.  
__Sie geht heim._

| Bestimmter Artikel          | Unbestimmter Artikel          |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Masculine/Femininum/Neutrum | der jungen Leute	junger Leute |

The **masculine nouns which require _-n/en_** for the accusative case have the **same forms** for the genitive case and no _-s_

_den Studenten, des Studenten;  
__den Jungen, des Jungen_

The genitive case is used for certain prepositions, the most important ones being _**während**, **wegen**_ and _**trotz** (inspite of)_. In colloquial German, you use the dative with these prepositions instead.

_während der Reise_ (during the trip)  
_wegen der Musik_ (because of the music)  
_trotz dieses Problems_ (inspite of this problem)

The genitive is also used in certain fixed expressions like _eines Tages_ or _meines Wissens (as far as I know)_.

# Passiv
To construct the passive form in German, you use a form of **_werden_ plus the past participle**:

_Er wird gegessen_. (He is eaten.)  
_Es wird geändert_. (It is changed.)

If you want to add an **agent** you use _von_ + dative (the English _by_). If the agent is not a person, _durch_ + accusative is used instead:

_Du wurdest von ihm gesehen._ (You were seen by him.)  
_Er wurde durch ein Wunder gerettet._ (He was saved by a miracle.)

If a phrase in the passive voice has **no subject**, an **impersonal _es_** can be used as the subject:

_Es wird daran gearbeitet._ (It is being worked on.)

**Dative forms** keep their form in the passive voice. They cannot become the subject but you can add _es_ in this case too.

_Es wird **dir** geholfen._ (You get help.)

Sometimes, the passive voice can be used for orders:

_Jetzt wird nicht geredet!_ (No one is talking now!)
